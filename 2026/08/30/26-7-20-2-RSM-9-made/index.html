【RSM】 Chapter 9 Made It

发表于2026-08-29|更新于2026-09-01|艺术文学拙作Reminiscences de Snowflake Mapleleaf
|浏览量:

Flip.

Twilight’s “library”, in the crystal castle. The room was fully filled with silence, too full to only hear the breath, along with the flipping sound of the pages.

Twilight was crouching in a bean bag, with a book placed open on the floor. Her sight moved along the lines, sometimes paused at some words. She casually levitated herself a piece of paper and a quill and wrote.

The weather went back to a sunny day. Sunlight poured on the floor, highlighting few dust wandering in the air.

Snowflake was sitting in another bean bag on the other side of the light. Her book buddy had just recommended her a book called Star Swirl’s Anecdotes. She laid the book on her lap, and looked at the page with her left hoof supporting her chin.

The old unicorn looked at the broccoli in the plate, then at the white alicorn opposite the table, “Come on, Celestia, you must be hungry, you have to eat!”

“Nuh-uh!” the alicorn shoot a … at one … piece, and … into …

She stared at that paragraph, the first few words went into her mind, then escaped. She retried from the start, then forget the old unicorn’s name. The characters just stands there, meaning nothing to her. She was even not quite sure she’s really “reading”—she just let her self facing the pages, pretending she was taking in. She didn’t know what she was thinking. Or rather, everything was there at once, and none of them was fully visible. The class, the empty lecture table, 25 pairs of eyes, and Mrs. Rich. Those thoughts kept bumping, like a group of moths, crashing in her brain.

She decided to change her posture. She picked up the book and slid lying on the bean bag, holding the book above her face.

The time ticked. Snowflake’s hooves were getting tired.

Oof!

Not a big sound, the book fell on her face, the paper hit her on her nose, with an aged smell of old fabric cover.

Twilight raised her head, the awkward scenery made her lifted herself up with her front legs.

“Are you OK?”

“I’m fine! It’s fine.” Snowflake got up, moved the book from her face, and touched her nose, “Not hurt.”

Twilight didn’t answer for instance but kept watching Snowflake’s face. She saw Snowflake’s lips let out a smile, but as Snowflake’s hooves quickly neaten her mane, she also saw something else…

“I mean really…” Twilight asked again, with a softer voice, “are you ok? You seem bothered.”

“Ah? Why?”

“’Cause you had just started at page 105.” Twilight pointed at the book in front of Snowflake, “An hour later, you were still at page 105. If Star Swirl’s stories were that interesting, I’ve got more.”

Snowflake checked the page. It was page 105. She didn’t know how she got here, probably she just turned to here randomly, and it happened to be this page.

“Oh… I… uh…”

Snowflake wanted to say something, but thoughts squeezed again in her throat. She couldn’t say a single word, or rather she didn’t know what to say. The air wasn getting heavier during the soundless gap.

Twilight returned to the book, like the conversation hadn’t existed. She turned one page again, leaving Snowflake in silence, staring at her. Minutes later, she continued as if having a new chat.

“However, this Romance of Lunagrad you gave me is quite awesome as well.” She levitated to wrote again, “I have to read carefully. A very romantic story, no wonder why you collect it.”

She noticed you.

Snowflake’s hooves dug into bean bag’s fabric, pressing a shallow pit on it. Her breath turned slower, because she felt something she cannot resist was rising up. She didn’t know what it was. But it was already past the point where she could push it back down.

She counted her breath, in…

Three.

Two.

One.

“I’m gonna lose my job.”

Her voice was low, her tone was calm. But the moment she spoke it out, there was a drop of sweat on her forehead, rolled down towards the hair on the temples, dropped on the bag’s velcet, and spread out a dark stain. She looked at that small stain, which confirmed the sentence did come from her.

Then her body started twitching. Starting from her shoulder, then her front hooves, then all over her body. She tried at any cost to push back that upward surge, making her swallowed with painful sound. She was not sure whether she’s crying or gasping, might be both.

Twilight stood up from the bean bag, trotted through the light, and sat down in front of Snowflake. She leaned in, with a front hoof bypassed Snowflake’s shoulder, and drew Snowflake near to her. Another hoof was slightly patting on Snowflake’s back, pat, pat, like waves softly pressing the beach.

“Easy,” Twilight’s voice was full of warmth, right beside Snowflake’s ear, “easy.”

Snowflake buried her face into Twilight’s chest fluff, her hooves were holding tightly on Twight’s shoulder. She heard her gasp was even heavier, the squeez in her throat was finally leaking out. Broken, disrupted words gushed out from the crack.

“What should I do, what should I do! The class will start tomorrow… but I can do NOTHING!”

She spoke faster, louder and louder, like a door that had been blocked for a long time was finally broke open, emotions inside rushed out.

“Why me! Why have to be ME! I’ve worked so hard… I’ve worked so hard but why nothing CHANGED!”

Then she paused, took a breath, the tension relieved a little, the next sentence was dragged out from the last remaining power from the deepest part of her body.

“I can’t lose my job… Not even death… I can’t go home… I can’t…”

The voice echoed under the vast ceiling, staying with the other words she had said, not lighter, not heavier.

Her shoulder stopped trembling, but she was still gasping, but the breath had turned slower and steadier than before. She relrased Twilight’s shoulder, little by little.

She raised her head.

Twilight’s face was near from hers. Her eyes were as calm as windless lakes, reflecting Snowflake’s exhausted look.

“Feeling better?” Her hooves had stopped patting, but still stayed on Snowfalke’s back.

Snowflake took a deep breath in, then breathe out. She could feel the air flowing through her chest. She nodded.

Twilight’s lips bent. She got the signal.

“You made it.” Twilight said.

Then she released Snowflake, stood up, walked back to her bean bag, sat down, and reopened Romance of Lunagrad.

“Let’s continue our reading, shall we?”

The room returned to silence again. All things returned to normal again, like…

Nothing… happend?

Snowflake got on the street, once again.

Like yesterday, she stood still, watching ponies living their lives. Unlike yesterday, all other chaos shut silent, with only one thought speaking out loud.

She couldn’t understand the kirin she was five minutes ago.

She remembered she decided, she cried, she did the last things she would ever allowed herself to do. She couldn’t remember when she had left, or whether she had said goodbye. Whatever the fact was, she could do nothing about them anymore.

Snowflake shook her head, clearing up her mind. As the sun went down, the air was getting cooler. A sense of moisture rose up with the smell of damp soil.

She suddenly recalled she left her book in the castle.

Never mind.

She started walking, still without a destination, but she kept walking.

The fountain square still remained some ponies closing stalls and shops. Opposite the road, she saw Apple Bloom helping her sister carrying apples back to the carriage, and it looked like Applejack had promised her something exciting. Snowflake stopped to wait for the carriages passing. Scootaloo on her scooter hurried through her and said hello, Snowflake nodded back.

She walked by a shop, the sign said “Quills & Sofas”. The shopwindow displayed a row of quills, whose colors varied in white and black, like piano keys. All nibs pointed downwards, ready to write anytime. Snowflake looked into the glass, her reflection joined the quill row as another displayed item. She went on.

Snowflake walked on a stone bridge. The water beneath was shallow and clear. The cobblestones were round, with colors of grey and brown, polished by the water for many years. On the other side of bridge stood an old store, with its wooden sign losing most of the paint, only remaining the words clear to be seen. It says “Old Books”. A yellow smell of antique papers surrounded Snowflake outside.

Not today.

Snowflake forgot the time, but the day didn’t wait. When the twilight was getting darker, the whole town was dyed with the Blues Moment. Snowflake passed by a gap between two houses, and the sinking light swept past her face.

Никого не будет в доме,
No one will be in the house -
Кроме сумерек. Один
Only twilight’s random shapes.
Зимний день в сквозном проеме
Wintry day will calmly browse
Незадернутых гардин.
Through half-open window drapes.

1

The melody surfaced in Snowflake’s mind, accompanied by the breeze pushing her forward. She didn’t know how did the idea came up, but somehow she admitted the song fits the moment well.

Then she turned left at the clock tower. The tower stood in the middle of the roundabout, also in Ponyville’s public memory. The spire was dark green, beneath which was the yellow clock face, with black pointers slowly spinning on it. Snowflake raised her head and stopped, then left with a moss-flavored impression.

Dew Florist’s

The smell was different. A mixed taste of flowers was chorusing a wide harmony in the air. The light was still on, the warm light lit up an area outside the shop, some broken sticks lied on the ground.

Snowflake went in.

Roseluck was at the corner of the shop, cleaning up the floor with a broom in her hooves. Her light yellow body and raspberry-colored mane were vaguely visible behind the bouquets of roses. She looked up while the bell rang by the opening door. She said “welcome” and went on.

Snowflake was wandering. She knew nothing about flowers except their names. She slightly lowered her head under a plum, and carefully avoid a leaf pile taken up half of the path.

The harmony was still continuing in Snowflake’s mind, she wanted to find out the singer singing in the alto.

Snowflake paused in front of a shelf of bottles, containing different fragrances. Each bottle was capped with a cork, and beside which was a small card with the name of the fragrance written on it—the font looked varied, might had been written at different times. Snowflake picked up a sample, and moved it near to her nose.

Lavender. The scent starts off very faintly rising up, then spreads out, carrying a hint of the astringency of herbs. She puts it back and picks up the bottle beside it. Rose. It’s sweet, sweet in a somewhat artificial way, as if saying “I’m fragrant” rather than “I’m a rose”.

Her sight finally pulled up at a group of orange bottles.

She picked the sample, moved it near to her nose, and the smell first tentatively explored the air, then gradually spread out—it was orange. Not the sweet and cloying scent of the candy store, but a cleaner, more fresh smell like that of a freshly peeled orange, with a hint of acidity and the roughness of the peel. But the base note was warm, like the warmth of the winter sun on the skin. She held the sample and sniffed it for a while longer, confirming that this was the right scent.

Orange fragrance, of course.

She took the bottle of orange fragrance and walked to the counter, placing it on the counter top. Roseluck glanced behind the bouquet, looked at the bottle, and then at Snowflake. She gave the price and then took the coin handed to her. She wrapped the bottle in a light brown paper—the paper was thin and had a rose with dew pattern on both sides—and then tied a knot in the middle with a thin hemp rope.

“Thank you,” she said, “have a nice evening.”

Snowflake took the paper package, pushed open the glass door, and returned to the street. The doorbell rang again, more softly than when she entered.

By the time Snowflake returned to music hall, the sky had been completely dark, not even the edges of the clouds.

She went in through the side door. The door hinge mourned a low, grating sound when opened, like the door hadn’t been opened from this side for a long time. The corridor was filled with nothing but faint light from the wall lamps. She passed by Liszt’s portrait, and catch a glimpse of the frame corner. She could only distinguish a unicorn’s figure, but not the appearance. She didn’t stop. The only thing she wanted to do was lying down.

Her steps paused at the stairwell.

Rhythmic sound came from the hall, sounding like… music. It went through serveral doors and the corridor, the volume had been significantly weakened. But her ears still caught it. A harmonica sang a prolonged sound, then another note answered, in a tone of violin. The clarinet joined in harmony, then the flute finally carried out the main theme. They’re assembling a melody Snowflake had never heard, filling in rhythm but somewhat feeling casual, like a group were having a conversation without using words. Each one finished their sentence, and then waited for the next one to continue.

Snowflake really needed her room. She had walked for a whole afternoon, her legs were heavy and her body was loose. But the music was keeping her downstairs.

She went back through the corridor. The door was exactly the place letting out the music, it wasn’t completely closed. The light and the gap projected a line on the ground.

She slightly pushed the door. The gap widened a little, giving Snowflake the whole scenery inside.

Only some spotlights on the stage were lit. The light came from high above, placing long shadows of the players on the ground. No one was in seats, the participants sat in a semi-circle on the stage, and instruments were randomly placed around them. A concert grand stood on the left, two violins were at the position slightly to the right of the center. A cello was on the right, and the clarinet and the harmonica were placed between them. No one was conducting, no one was even at the center. They just confirmed by eye contact, then started together.

Snowflake leaned against the locked side of the door, spared a hoof to hold the fragrance bag, but leaving her eyes looking in.

They were not professionals, Snowflake could hear that. Some notes were not accurate, a beat was lagged for a half, the harmony between clarinet and cello failed but then reconnected. But the ensemble still went on steadily, the music flowed like stream running on the stony bed.

Snowflake had been living in this music hall for quite a long time. How time flies! But as time went by, Snowflake started forgetting she was living in a music hall. Or furthermore, she hadn’t listen to some music like a real audience—just listen, embracing the magic directly from the sound—for a long time. She started learning to play piano when she was 8. She must have become a music kirin much earlier than that, or she wouldn’t have that interest and courage to delve into transcendental music theories.

She listened, as her eyelids gradually closed. Her heartbeat started leaving the previous pace, and swang as the rhythm compressing and expanding.

She didn’t have her notebook beside, not to mention tonality analysis and chord progression. Wrong note? That’s performers’ own style. The music went into her ears, they’re just music, nothing else.

A piece ended. Someone applauded, the applause echoed in the hall as if in a valley. Then the next piece started. It was that harmonica again. This time the music started with a rapid running melody, then other instruments joined with increasing intensity.

Snowflake didn’t know how long had the performance lasted. She finally woke up when she heard they putting the instruments into the cases, the spotlights started going off one by one, and talking performers started walking out of the hall. Reluctantly, Snowflake gently pushed herself away from the door and went back to the corridor.

As she climbed up the wooden staircase, faint creaking sound came up under her hooves. The noise was not loud, but it was clear in the empty stairwell, as if each step was declaring, “You’re back.”

She pushed open the door of the attic, without turning on the light. The moonlight streamed in through the window, creating a narrow silver path between the bed and the floor. She placed the paper package on the bedside table and sat down by the bed.

She took a deep breath in, then slowly breathed out.

She sat for a while, looking at the light brown paper package on the cabinet, the shadow of the fine hemp rope stretching out very long under the moonlight. She thought about all the things that had happened today—the words she said in the library, the bottles she picked up and put down in the florist’s, the tune of the harmonica she heard in the concert hall.

Go to bed.
