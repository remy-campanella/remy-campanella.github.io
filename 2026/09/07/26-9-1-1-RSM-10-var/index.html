【RSM】 Chapter 10 Impromptu Variation in C Major

发表于2026-09-06|更新于2026-09-06|艺术文学拙作Reminiscences de Snowflake Mapleleaf
|浏览量:

By the time the bell rang, Snowflake had arrived at the classroom door. She pushed into the room, stood by the lecture table and turned. Students were all in their seats, with their textbooks prepared on their desks. She ran her eyes over them. They were watching her, waiting for something she should bring, including the one sitting at the back. Mrs. Rich was there, pen on a tablet, tablet in her hoof, looking at her expressionlessly.

Snowflake brought nothing.

Except the fact she was still alive this morning.

She took a deep breath, then breathed out long, wasting time for about 30 seconds. Some students turned back and give a glance to Mrs. Rich. Mrs. Rich was writing, the pen tip moved on the paper, making a rustling sound. Definitely not something pleasant.

Snowflake turned right, moving towards the piano.

She had sat on a piano stool for many times. But this time, she felt nothing is blocking between the stool and herself. She opened the lid. In front of her were interlcaced black and white keys, no matter how the music varies, these won’t change.

She pushed her right hoof on the keys, moving from the lowest to the highest, then again. Her hoof passed by the keys, passed through the sunlight poured down.

Then she played a C major chord.

The perfect harmony suddenly filled up all gaps in the room. Then she started carrying out a melody in andante. The right hoof plays the theme, and the left hoof gave accompaniment surronding the base note. The melody was brand new, even for Snowflake herself, but it sounds like it had waited for her for a long time.

The class began to lose their composure. Some of them looked at each other, discussing whether their teacher had gone insane. The voice was low, but still hearable in the classroom. Snowflake didn’t stop, letting the theme reach its distination several bars later, then end on a clean long note.

A minor.

The melody came back, starting in a higher register. The theme is fragmented, like the sound of a flute drifting in the wind. The rhythm is stretched out, with long notes lingering and short notes passing by quickly, like a shepherd boy playing a short flute on the grassland. She hadn’t seen any grassland, but she knows what it should be looked like. It is a place where the sea of grass meets the sky on the horizon, and when the wind blows, the grass waves layer by layer towards the distance. Cows and sheep lower their heads to eat grass, and the shepherd boy stands on a gentle slope, the flute sound spreading out from his position, blown apart by the wind, then reassembled by the wind again. The lingering notes of the theme linger in the alto range for a moment, and then naturally fall into the second variation.

No one talked anymore. Mrs. Rich’s pen hadn’t moved for a long time.

Variation 2. G major, played more softly and tentatively, hanging around the alto. A new theme came in from the treble, then coming down in a spiral motion. Two melodies slowly getting together under the tree shadow. A young mare was hiding behind the tree, observing the sheperd boy, and so did him. Two melodies were seperated at first, walking their own ways, then getting near, crossing, and twisting. He invited for a dance, she said yes. Then a waltz landed in a segmented rhythm, rapsodic but still with elegance. The emphasis of three-four time was intentionally misplaced. Like the bodies was pushed away but then softly pulled back.

The students were moving. They opened the textbook, turning pages in a hurry. “Is this e minor?” “No, it’s G major.” Discussions spreaded along with page sound, even Diamond Tiara is looking at what others were looking at.

Snowflake didn’t stop, her sight switches from keys to keys.

There was almost no pause. Several accents fell like thunder, splitting the waltz rhythm in half. This is variation 3. A storm was coming. The percussive sounds in the bass register were like distant rolling thunder, while the right hoof moved rapidly in the high register, resembling the sound of raindrops hitting the grassland. The shepherd called the cattle and sheep to run towards the tent, but the mare did not leave. She ran up and, together with the shepherd’s sheepdog, guided the lead sheep through the low-lying area. The two melodies alternated and interwove in the storm, supporting each other. The rain sounds gradually retreated to the bass register, turning into a continuous, deep rumbling. Then the C major theme reappeared in the high register, not loud, but firm, like a lamp still shining outside the tent in the storm. The shepherd and the girl sat side by side inside the tent, the firelight reflecting on their sides. The melody stopped on a gentle long note, without concluding, just pausing.

Then came the fourth variation. The theme began in the key of F major, resonant and warm, as if heard by the entire grassland. The chord progressions stacked layer upon layer, like the sound of bells. They held a wedding on the grassland. The original theme was stretched and widened, like a river flowing into the plain, its speed slowing down but its depth increasing. The melody ascended on an ascending scale, one note after another moving upwards without hesitation, without any room for turning back, nd then fell straight down, landing on a complete and clean cadence.

Snowflake’s hooves left the keys. The vibration of the piano string remain for seconds, then faded away.

The whole class fell silence for a minute, nobody moved, no body talked. Mrs. Rich’s pen was still there, being forgotten to be put down.

Then it burst hoof applause, from behind every desk.

Snowflake sat still on the piano stool. She felt her heart was beating soundly and steadily in her chest. A drop of sweat fell down as her breath waved. Then the bell went off again. In laughs and cheers, students left the classroom one by one. Snowflake moved on a while later. She took a rag and cleaned the keys, the closed the piano lid and wipe its surface. By the time she finished that, all the others were all gone. Snowflake sighed. She stood up, went through the classroom door, then turned off the lights.

“Snowflake.”

She stopped and turned, it was Mis. Rich. Mrs. Rich was there not far away, with the tablet in her hoof, complex emotion on her face.

“It was a terrible lesson.” Mrs. Rich said.

Snowflake looked at the tablet. Course topic, none. Course introduction, none. Knowledge point, none. Some tiny characters were written at the corner, Snowflake couldn’t read them.

“But my daughter wasn’t sleeping. Students were all… how should I say… ‘operating’.”

Snowflake didn’t know how to continue properly. “I think it’s a… good thing?”

Mrs. Rich’s lips bent a little upwards, “Ha.” Then she moved her sights on Snowflake’s hooves.

“Nice music today, isn’t it? Who wrote it? May I see the score?”

“I’m afraid you couldn’t, madam,” Snowflake leaned her body back a little, “it’s an impromptu.”

Mrs. Rich paused for a second, “Impromptu? You… made it up?”

“According to the definition of impromptu, yes.”

Mrs. Rich paused again, her hoof slightly tapped on the tablet. She looked to the window, then quickly back to Snowflake’s eyes.

“You’re an amazing composer.”

“Thanks, madam.”

“However,” Mrs. Rich went on, with a tone much serious, much familiar to Snowflake, “your job is a music teacher, your students need to take test. Your future classes are not supposed to go too far from the examination outline.”

Future classes? Which means…

“Ah yes,” said Snowflake, “thanks madam, I’ll remember that.”

Mrs. Rich nodded, then she left. Snowflake could hear her hoofsteps gradually faded away down the corridor towards the exit, and then disappeared.

Then another sequence of hoofsteps appeared, much faster. Cheerilee sticked out her head behind the door frame.

“I heard them all in the office!” She said, “You go, girl! You’ve just shut Mrs. Rich up!”

Snowflake slightly waved, “No no, it’s not finished yet. Mrs. Rich wants three classes a week, not three concerts.”

“Alright, alright. You’re always so humble.”

“Really? Then who is the general teacher before I get here?”

“Okay, seriously now.” Cheerilee said, “I want you to see this.” She hooved out a leaflet. It was folded, with slightly curled corners, stuffed into Snowflake’s hoof.

Snowflake unfolded it. There was lines of texts on it.


Friendship School
Opening Day
All sectors of society are welcomed to come and communicate.

“Friendship Sckool’s opening day?” Snowflake raised her head.

“Exactly.” Cheerilee answered, “When it comes to shutting up, nothing beats when Twilight shut down Chancellor Neighsay.”

“I’m not learning shutting others down,” Snowflake folded the leaflet back, “I need to study there teaching method.”

Cheerilee didn’t respond. She merely patted Snowflake on her shoulder, giving it a mischievous look, and then turned and walked back.

The corridor fell silent. Snowflake stood by the window, with the sunlight falling on her back, warm and just right. She glanced down at her front hooves, the ones that had just played an entire improvisational piece. There was no sweat, no tremor, no marks. Only herself, and a tiny bit of autumn afternoon light resting on them.
