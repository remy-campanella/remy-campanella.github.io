【RSM】 Chapter 11 Good Times

发表于2026-10-11|更新于2026-10-12|艺术文学拙作Reminiscences de Snowflake Mapleleaf
|浏览量:

*Ring*

The bell rang with joy. Snowflake walked into Hay Café.

“Morning, Miss Snowflake!” Still that waiter stallion, still that warm smile, “Shmoopy Pie and Ponyville Shimmers, as usual?”

Snowflake blinked in surprise. “You remember that?”

“As a famous café in town, we do get tons of orders every day.” The waiter replied, “But for our old friends, we simply cannot forget.”

Snowflake’s gaze drifted away., “But… I haven’t been here for quite a long time.”

“That’s exactly why we remember you so well,” The waiter let out a laugh, “we even wondered whether you’d given up eating breakfast altogether. But since you’ve come back, that doesn’t matter anymore.”

Snowflake blushed. She did forget to eat breakfast quite often lately. He was right, the past is in the past, she could at least let herself have breakfast properly from now on.

“Then I’ll take…” Snowflake glanced at the menu board, “… Caramel Apples!”

“All right, that’ll be four bits. Please take a seat, your meal is on the way!”

It wasn’t that she no longer liked Ponyville Shimmers, it was just her stomach had simply made the decision for her this morning. Kind of interesting, maybe she could let her stomach choose breakfast from now on.

Snowflake sat at a long table facing the window. The meal arrived, and Snowflake was eating leisurely while watching creatures passing by. Shops were opening one by one, several students ran past the window with their schoolbags on their backs, including an overslept filly dashing with a piece of bread held in her mouth.

There was nothing special about the street.

But she had plenty of time to watch.

The sunlight crept along the street little by little. As the crystal spike started reflecting the morning light, Snowflake had nearly finished the apples on her plate. She held up the cup, draining the last of her coffee.

“Good morning, Miss Snowflake.”

Snowflake’s head turned.

Sitting beside her was a brownish-grey donkey. The golden hair on top of his head made himself look younger, but the look in his eyes and the sound of his voice betrayed his age, which made Snowflake somewhat doubtful about the hair itself. Snowflake didn’t remember ever meeting him, let alone when they had become familiar enough for him to greet her so casually.

“He… Hello?”

The old donkey laughed. “Don’t worry, Miss, I’m not a villain. My name is Cranky Doodle1. I live at the west end of this street. I know who you are, thanks to Pinkie. However, I think we’ve met before.”

Snowflake was even more confused. “We’ve met before?”

“Of course we did. But that depends on how good your memory is.” Cranky picked up his coffee, taking a slow sip, “Perhaps you’ll remember… the harmonica?”

Harmonica?

Snowflake’s body trembled slightly.

The music hall in the evening, the half-closed door, and that little band put together on the spot all came rushing back to her. She was in the shadow behind the door, with only a narrow sliver of the room visible through the gap. She was holding a bottle of orange fragrance, and it was still on her desk.

Snowflake’s ears gradually drooped down.

“You… know I was watching?”

“Yes. I was facing at the door when I was playing.”

Snowflake sat up straight at once. Why had she hidden behind the door that day? Surely it would have been fine to walk in. Although the concert had been far from formal, she should at least have let them know she was there. Sneaking around to listen to someone else’s performance was impolite no matter how she looked at it.

“In my humble opinion,” Cranky’s words interrupted Snowflake’s daydreaming, “You looked like a little sun standing there. That tiny gap couldn’t hide you.”

“Well…” Snowflake hesitated, her mouth half-open.

“No, it’s not as complicated as you think.” Cranky slightly waved his right hoof, “we just love music, so we get together and play. We meet in the music hall every Sunday night. You can come if you want.”

Snowflake raised her head. “I can come too?”

“The door isn’t locked.”

“Oh.” She paused, her ears perking up a little. “Thank you.”

Cranky nodded, taking another sip of coffee. They both fell silent, and Snowflake thought the conversation was over.

“Speaking of which,” Cranky started again, “you’re the only kirin I’ve ever met here. Did you come to Ponyville by yourself?”

“Yes.”

“Oh, that can’t have been easy.” Cranky’s eyes slightly widened, “Living somewhere new means starting over from scratch.”

Snowflake blinked. Before she could reply, she noticed something too.

“Donkeys seem pretty rare around here too. Apart from you, I only know Matilda.”

Cranky straightened slightly. “She’s my wife.”

“Oh!” Snowflake’s ears stood up at once, her tail swished excitedly.

“Celestia bless! It must be wonderful to have a family here, to have each other’s company for decades.” Then she looked down, fiddling with the cup in front of her. “Unlike me, theoretically if I don’t have a class to teach, I might not meet any creature all day, even not saying a single word.”

“Actually there hasn’t been decades. We’ve only been married for three years.”

Snowflake was taken aback. “Why did you… um… marry so late?”

“That was many years ago, at the Grand Galloping Gala,” Cranky said, with the pride of someone showing off his greatest treasure, “She’s the best donkey, or even the best creature I’ve ever met. After the gala I went looking for her, but she left, and I have no idea where she had gone.”

“And then?” Snowflake turned to face him fully.

“I swore I’d find her no matter what. I became a miserable traveller, from town to town, city to city, all over Equestria.”

“Did you find her?”

“No.”

That was not the answer she’d expected, but Cranky didn’t keep her waiting for long.

“Finally I gave up, I came to Ponyville and settled down.”

Snowflake knitted her brows. Her gaze drifted aside for a few seconds, then returned to Cranky.

“But… Matilda has lived here for a long time.”

Cranky threw up his hooves. “Yeah, surprise!”

Snowflake looked at him, feeling unsure whether to laugh or feel sorry for him. Searching the entire Equestria, he finally discovered that the creature he had been looking for had been living right where he had ended up. It sounded like the kind of story where the author was deliberately teasing the protagonist.

“Then did Matilda still recognise you?”

“I was in my house, locking all doors and windows, because Pinkie Pie was trying to break in to ‘make friends’.”

Snowflake couldn’t help but chuckle, same old Pinkie Pie.

“Matilda was outside, and she recognised me by my voice. For a moment, I couldn’t believe it was really her.”

“If I were you, I wouldn’t have believed it either. Everything suddenly working out like that? Who would?”

Cranky slowly turned the cup between his hooves.

“Wrong answer, kid.”

Snowflake’s smile faltered. “Wh… What?”

“The wedding shouldn’t have been that day. But the invitations had the wrong date on them. By the time we noticed, we were supposed to get married in just a few hours.”

“Oh…” Snowflake rubbed her hooves together. Cranky continued drinking coffee.

“Oh… So that’s how it is.”

She had already come up with a grand finale for the story: the bride, the red carpet, and the old but warm-hearted gentleman donkey in a neat suit. But now she had to leave that version for fairy tales.

Snowflake fell silent for a while, then she continued.

“Then what?”

“Get married.”

“I mean the wedding.”

“Did as the invitation said.”

“In a few hours?”

“Of course.”

“All of them?”

“Uh huh.”

Snowflake leaned a little closer.

“The venue, the flowers, the music… All in a few hours?”

Cranky finally noticed the excitement in her voice. “Miss, you seem quite interested in that.”

Snowflake leaned back at once. “No, I just…”

But she had already given herself away.

“Fine, maybe a little.”

“Why is that?”

“You know, music, Romanticism… Well Romanticism isn’t the same thing as romance, but it did spark my interest into that.”

Cranky looked at her, then picked up the cup again.

“Then ask Matilda for the details later.”

“Why?”

“All that travelling wore me out. Matilda probably remembers the details better than I do.”

Snowflake smiled, “OK!”

Cranky finished his coffee, then got up from his chair. “I have to leave, I have to go. Matilda asked me to pick up a few supplies.”

Snowflake looked at the clock. Breakfast had lasted much longer than she’d expected.

Cranky reached the door, then looked back.

“Sunday night?”

Snowflake froze. “What?”

“The music hall.”

“Oh, right.”

“Come if you want.” Cranky opened the door. “Don’t sneak behind the door.”

Snowflake went red. “I won’t!”

*Ring!*

He left.

Snowflake continued stayed by the table for a moment. Snowflake remained at the table for a moment. Out of habit, she picked up her cup. Just before it reached her lips, she realised it was empty.

Romance.

The word whirred around in her mind.

Snowflake suddenly put down the cup.

“Romance of Lunagrad!”

“Finally you remembered.”

Twilight’s horn sparkled, and Romance of Lunagrad floated down from a bookshelf in a purple aura. She gently placed it in front of Snowflake.

Snowflake caught it immediately. “I’ve just had a few things going on lately.”

“A few?”

Snowflake pretended not to hear that. She flipped through the book to check.. “Is my bookmark still there?”

“It is.”

“Okay.”

Twilight returned to her desk and spread out the files she’d just set down. “You’ve forgotten about it for long enough to give me just enough time to finish it.”

Snowflake lifted her head. “You’ve finished?”

“Uh-huh.”

“How’s the ending?”

Twilight continued sorting documents, “Find out for yourself.”

“Good ending or bad ending?”

“Find out for yourself.”

“Were they still alive?”

Twilight looked back, “Snowflake, please.”

“Fine.”

Snowflake slipped the book into her saddlebag, muttering, “So mean.”

“Thank you.”

Snowflake turned to leave. Her hoof reached the door handle, but she didn’t open the door.

Twilight turned a page.

The door still hadn’t opened. Twilight looked back again and saw Snowflake standing there with her back to her.

“Something wrong?”

Snowflake’s ears moved. She took a deep breath, then slowly turned around.

“Well… I’m very sorry.”

Twilight was taken aback. “Sorry for what?”

Snowflake ruffled her mane and looked away toward the bookshelf., “The dinner, and our ‘book time’ after that.”

Twilight paused for a second, then her eyes widened. “Oh, you mean that.”

Snowflake nodded. “Yes.”

“Aww, it’s alright.”

“But I fell asleep in front of you, in front of Luna!”

“Luna wasn’t angry.”

Snowflake studied Twilight’s face. “She wasn’t?”

“She wasn’t.” Twilight replied with a smile, “You just left her with the same impression everyone else had of you.”

“Which was?”

“Stressed out.”

Snowflake blinked twice. “Just… stressed out?”

“You were sleeping so deeply that night.” Twilight replied, “We carried you back to your attic, and you slept the whole way. I think we could even have thrown you through the window.”

Snowflake gasped. “You didn’t actually do that, right?”

“No.”

What a relief.

“That was Spike’s idea.”

“Twilight!”

Twilight burst out laughing. Snowflake stared at her, trying to maintain the last bit of seriousness. Eventually, she couldn’t hold it any longer, and the corners of her mouth curled upward.

After the laughter died down, Snowflake lingered. She looked at the ground, then at Twilight. “So… everyone knew I was under pressure?”

Twilight thought for a while, “Well, I haven’t done any strict research on that.”

Snowflake started to nod.

“But you don’t really think Fluttershy brought you cupcakes because she had so many she couldn’t finish them herself, do you?”

Snowflake paused. She opened her mouth, but no words came out. She lowered her head, and traced the carpet lightly with the tip of her hoof. “… Okay.”

The scattered pieces in Snowflake’s memory suddenly began to fit together. She didn’t follow the thought any further; she only let out a slow breath.

“I really… didn’t know what I was doing back then.”

Twilight didn’t answer. Silence settled between them for a moment.

“After crying that morning,” Twilight asked, “did you feel better?”

“Yes.” Snowflake met her gaze this time. “I felt better.”

Two seconds passed.

“Thank you.”

“You’re welcome.” Twilight smiled.

“So… I’m leaving now.” Snowflake opened the door.

“Don’t leave your book behind again.”

“I won’t!”

“And Sunday night?”

Snowflake stopped halfway through the doorway.

“What for?”

“Your concert.”

“How do you know?”

“Pinkie.”

Snowflake’s eyes rolled, “… Of course.”
