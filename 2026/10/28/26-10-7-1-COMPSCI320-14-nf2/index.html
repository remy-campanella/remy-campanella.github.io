【COMPSCI 320】 Lecture 14 Network Flow 2

发表于2026-10-28|更新于2026-10-28|技术学习COMPSCI 320 Applied Algorithms

Revision Focus

The 2023 maze question and 2025 matching/vertex-disjoint-path questions make modelling and recovering an actual solution central. For every reduction, specify vertices, arcs, capacities, the meaning of one unit of flow, both correctness directions, integrality, and output recovery.

Bipartite Matching

Definitions

A matching is a set of edges with no shared endpoint. A maximum matching has maximum cardinality; a maximal matching merely cannot be enlarged by adding one edge and need not be maximum.

A graph is bipartite if its vertices split into \(L\) and \(R\) with all edges crossing between them.

Max-Flow Formulation

Add source \(s\) and sink \(t\). Give every arc capacity one:

  • \(s\to u\) for each \(u\in L\).
  • \(u\to v\) for each original edge \(uv\) with \(u\in L,v\in R\).
  • \(v\to t\) for each \(v\in R\).

Proof of Correctness and Recovery

A matching of size \(k\) gives an integral flow of value \(k\) by sending one unit through each matched pair. Conversely, an integral flow has at most one selected pair incident to each vertex, because of the unit source/sink arcs. The \(L\to R\) arcs carrying flow one form a matching of the same cardinality.

Integer capacities guarantee that an integral maximum flow can be found, for example starting from zero with augmentations. An arbitrary fractional optimum should not be interpreted directly as a set of matched edges.

The network has \(|L|+|R|+2\) vertices and \(|E|+|L|+|R|\) arcs. Ordinary augmenting paths need at most \(\min\{|L|,|R|\}\) augmentations. A unit-network implementation gives the familiar \(O(|E|\sqrt{|V|})\) matching bound after preprocessing isolated vertices.

Hall’s Condition

When \(|L|=|R|\), a perfect matching exists iff every \(S\subseteq L\) satisfies \(|N(S)|\geq|S|\). Necessity follows because distinct matched vertices in \(S\) require distinct neighbors. The sufficiency follows from the flow/min-cut formulation. Do not test only single vertices; a larger subset may be deficient.

Disjoint Paths

Edge-Disjoint Paths

For a directed graph, give every original edge unit capacity and compute an integral maximum flow. Decompose it into \(s\to t\) unit paths, discarding flow cycles. Unit capacity prevents any edge from appearing in two paths.

Conversely, \(k\) edge-disjoint paths support a flow of value \(k\). Thus the maximum number of such paths equals the minimum number of edges whose deletion separates \(s\) from \(t\).

Internally Vertex-Disjoint Paths (2025 Q6(c))

Split every internal vertex \(v\) into \(v_{in}\) and \(v_{out}\) and add \(v_{in}\to v_{out}\) of capacity one. Replace each original edge \(u\to v\) by an arc from the out-copy of \(u\) to the in-copy of \(v\), retaining unsplit endpoints \(s,t\). Give these original-edge arcs capacity one as well.

Every unit path consumes the unit vertex arc of each internal vertex it visits. Conversely, internally vertex-disjoint paths give an integral flow. The endpoints may be shared and must not receive a capacity-one bottleneck. Unit original-edge capacity also prevents counting the same direct \(s\to t\) edge multiple times.

Recover unit paths by flow decomposition and replace each split pair by the original vertex. Do not confuse edge-disjointness with vertex-disjointness.

Minimum Maze Blockers (2023 SAQ2)

Remove existing obstacles and represent each remaining cell by a vertex. Connect horizontally or vertically adjacent cells in both directions. Split each cell other than \(s,t\) with a unit-capacity vertex arc. Give movement arcs capacity \(M\), where \(M\) exceeds the number of removable cells, and leave \(s,t\) unremovable.

A finite cheap cut therefore selects cell arcs, not movement arcs. Its capacity equals the number of newly blocked cells. The exam keeps the endpoints nonadjacent, so deleting internal cells can separate them.

Recover the cells. After max flow, let \(A\) be residual-reachable from \(s\). Select exactly those cells with \(v_{in}\in A\) and \(v_{out}\notin A\). Removing them blocks every maze path. Reporting only the cut value does not answer the request for the actual blocker set.

Extensions to Max Flow

Multiple Sources and Sinks

Add a super-source with arcs to all sources and a super-sink with arcs from all sinks. Their capacities encode source/sink limits. If no individual limit is intended, use a finite number larger than any possible total original flow. Keep original intermediate-vertex conservation.

Circulation with Supplies and Demands

Use the convention \[ \sum_{e\text{ into }v}f(e)-\sum_{e\text{ out of }v}f(e)=d(v). \] Thus \(d(v)>0\) means demand and \(d(v)<0\) means supply. Necessarily \(\sum_vd(v)=0\).

Add super-source \(s^*\) and super-sink \(t^*\). For each supply vertex, add \(s^*\to v\) with capacity \(-d(v)\); for each demand vertex, add \(v\to t^*\) with capacity \(d(v)\). Retain original capacities.

A feasible circulation exists iff the maximum flow equals \[ D=\sum_{v:d(v)>0}d(v), \] so every new supply/demand arc is saturated. Restricting this flow to original arcs recovers the required circulation.

Lower Bounds

Suppose \(\ell(e)\leq f(e)\leq c(e)\). Write \(f(e)=\ell(e)+g(e)\). The residual capacity for \(g\) is \(c(e)-\ell(e)\), and its required demand is \[ d'(v)=d(v)-\sum_{e\text{ into }v}\ell(e)+\sum_{e\text{ out of }v}\ell(e). \] Solve the resulting demand-circulation instance, then restore \(\ell(e)\). Reject if any \(\ell(e)>c(e)\). The signs follow from the chosen inflow minus outflow convention; using an opposite convention requires reversing them consistently.

To test an \(s\to t\) flow with lower bounds and ordinary internal conservation, add \(t\to s\) with sufficiently large capacity and test for a circulation. A lower bound on this new arc can require a minimum total flow value. Integer data admit an integral feasible solution whenever one exists.

Survey Design

Suppose each customer must answer between a lower and upper number of questions, and each product must receive between a lower and upper number of responses. Create arcs source-to-customer with those customer bounds, unit customer-to-product arcs for eligible pairs, and product-to-sink arcs with product bounds. Add sink-to-source and use lower-bound circulation.

An integral flow one on a customer-product arc assigns that question. Bounds enforce response quotas, and unit pair capacities prevent duplicate questions. This is a reusable quota-assignment model.

Airline Scheduling

Flight \(i\) departs \(o_i\) at time \(s_i\) and arrives at \(d_i\) at time \(f_i\). Put a compatibility arc \(i\to j\) if a crew can complete \(i\), transfer if necessary, and reach \(j\) before departure. With increasing flight times this forms a DAG.

Crew Reuse as a Path Cover

Make a bipartite copy of the flight set; connect \(i_L\to j_R\) for compatible successive flights. A matching selects at most one successor and predecessor per flight. Since the compatibility graph is acyclic, matched links form disjoint paths rather than cycles.

For \(k\) flights and a maximum matching of size \(q\), the minimum number of crews is \(k-q\): every selected link reuses a crew and reduces the number of paths by one. Recover flight chains from the matching.

The equivalent circulation model splits every flight with a mandatory unit lower-bound arc, adds compatibility arcs and source/sink arcs, and bounds the sink-to-source arc to test whether a given number of crews suffices. This simplified model assumes crews may start/end as allowed by these arcs; extra base or duty-time restrictions require additional modelling.

Image Segmentation

Assign each pixel to foreground \(A\) or background \(B\), with source in \(A\) and sink in \(B\). Let \(D_F(p)\) and \(D_B(p)\) be the costs of assigning pixel \(p\) foreground or background.

  • Add \(s\to p\) of capacity \(D_B(p)\).
  • Add \(p\to t\) of capacity \(D_F(p)\).
  • For neighboring pixels \(p,q\), add both directed arcs of capacity \(w_{pq}\geq0\).

A cut pays the correct unary assignment cost, plus \(w_{pq}\) once whenever the two neighbors get different labels. Therefore a minimum cut minimizes the total labelling penalty. Check the source/sink orientation; swapping unary capacities swaps the meaning of the labels.

Baseball Elimination

For team \(x\), let \(W=w_x+r_x\) be its maximum possible final wins. If some other team already has more than \(W\) wins, \(x\) is trivially eliminated. Otherwise build a network for games among the other teams:

  • Source-to-game \((i,j)\) capacity \(g_{ij}\), the number of remaining games.
  • Game-to-team \(i\) and \(j\) arcs with sufficiently large capacity.
  • Team \(i\)-to-sink capacity \(W-w_i\).

All source-game arcs can be saturated iff remaining wins can be assigned so no competitor exceeds \(W\). This version permits a tie for first place; requiring sole first changes the upper bounds to \(W-1-w_i\).

A failed saturation and a min cut identify a set of teams whose required wins cannot fit their combined allowances. Focus on why game vertices split wins between the two teams; the complete elimination-certificate proof is secondary.

Modelling Checklist

  • Is one unit a matched pair, a path, a crew, an assignment, or a win?
  • Are constraints on edges or vertices? Do you need vertex splitting?
  • Are there minimum quotas requiring lower bounds?
  • Why can an integral solution be chosen?
  • How does every feasible original solution give a flow, and vice versa?
  • What network sizes and max-flow algorithm give the claimed running time?
  • How do you translate the resulting flow or cut back into the requested objects?
