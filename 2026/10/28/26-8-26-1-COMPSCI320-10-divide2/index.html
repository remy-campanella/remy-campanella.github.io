【COMPSCI 320】 Lecture 10 Divide and Conquer 2

发表于2026-10-28|更新于2026-10-28|技术学习COMPSCI 320 Applied Algorithms
|浏览量:

Revision Focus

Past-paper evidence. The 2025 final Q3 asks five Master Theorem calculations, and Q4 asks for a logarithmic-time local-minimum algorithm with a proof. Earlier papers also test recurrences and FFT concepts. Prioritize deriving a recurrence and explaining the combine step; retain FFT’s representations, padding, and complexity before studying its implementation details.

These priorities summarize the 2022-2025 papers discussed in the COMPSCI 320 chat. They are not an official forecast of the next exam.

Master Theorem

Divide-and-Conquer Recurrence

For equal-size recursive subproblems, \[ T(n)=aT(n/b)+f(n), \] where \(a\geq1\) is the constant number of subproblems, \(b>1\) is the constant shrink factor, and \(f(n)\geq0\) is the non-recursive divide/combine work. Small inputs take \(\Theta(1)\) time.

For \(f(n)=\Theta(n^c)\), level \(i\) has \(a^i\) subproblems of size \(n/b^i\), so its total work is \[ \Theta\left(n^c(a/b^c)^i\right). \] There are \(\Theta(\log_b n)\) levels and \(\Theta(n^{\log_ba})\) leaves. Sum the geometric series, including the leaves.

Master Theorem

Let \(p=\log_ba\). For fixed \(a\geq1\), \(b>1\), and \(c\geq0\), \[ T(n)=aT(n/b)+\Theta(n^c) =\begin{cases} \Theta(n^c),&c>p,\\ \Theta(n^c\log n),&c=p,\\ \Theta(n^p),&c<p. \end{cases} \]

Root work dominates in the first case, all levels contribute equally in the second, and leaf work dominates in the third. Floors and ceilings do not change these orders under the usual constant-size base cases.

Exam method. Identify \(a,b,c\), compare \(a\) with \(b^c\), state the applicable case, and give a tight \(\Theta\) bound. Do not confuse an upper bound with a tight bound.

Recurrence (2025 Q3) Comparison Tight bound
\(P(n)=3P(n/2)+n^2\) \(3<2^2\) \(\Theta(n^2)\)
\(Q(n)=4Q(n/2)+n^2\) \(4=2^2\) \(\Theta(n^2\log n)\)
\(R(n)=5R(n/2)+n^2\) \(5>2^2\) \(\Theta(n^{\log_2 5})\)
\(S(n)=2S(n/4)+\sqrt n\) \(2=4^{1/2}\) \(\Theta(\sqrt n\log n)\)
\(U(n)=3U(n/4)+\sqrt n\) \(3>4^{1/2}\) \(\Theta(n^{\log_4 3})\)

Gaps in This Version of the Theorem

This polynomial-work version does not directly cover unequal recursive sizes, nonconstant branching factors, or work such as \(n\log n\). Use a recursion tree, substitution, or an appropriate stronger theorem instead.

Examples: \(T(n)=T(n/5)+T(7n/10)+O(n)\) needs a separate argument; \(T(n)=T(n-1)+n\) has depth \(n\), not \(\log n\).

Integer Multiplication

Addition and Subtraction

Adding or subtracting two \(n\)-bit integers takes \(\Theta(n)\) bit operations. Long multiplication takes \(\Theta(n^2)\) bit operations. The input length is the number of bits, not the numerical value of the integer.

Karatsuba’s Algorithm

Write \(x=2^ma+b\) and \(y=2^mc+d\), where \(m=\lceil n/2\rceil\).

A direct split uses four recursive products: \(ac,ad,bc,bd\). Karatsuba uses only three: \[ p=ac,\qquad q=bd,\qquad r=(a-b)(c-d). \] Since \(ad+bc=p+q-r\), \[ xy=2^{2m}p+2^m(p+q-r)+q. \] Shifts and additions cost \(O(n)\), giving \[ T(n)=3T(\lceil n/2\rceil)+O(n)=\Theta(n^{\log_2 3}). \] The exponent is about \(1.585\). The essential improvement is reducing the number of multiplications, not simply splitting the input.

Matrix Multiplication

Dot Product and the Standard Algorithm

A dot product of two length-\(n\) vectors takes \(\Theta(n)\) arithmetic operations. For two \(n\times n\) matrices, \[ c_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^n a_{ik}b_{kj}, \] so the standard algorithm takes \(\Theta(n^3)\) arithmetic operations.

Block Matrix Multiplication

Partition each matrix into four square blocks. The four output blocks are \[ \begin{aligned} C_{11}&=A_{11}B_{11}+A_{12}B_{21},&C_{12}&=A_{11}B_{12}+A_{12}B_{22},\\ C_{21}&=A_{21}B_{11}+A_{22}B_{21},&C_{22}&=A_{21}B_{12}+A_{22}B_{22}. \end{aligned} \] This uses eight half-size products and \(\Theta(n^2)\) addition work: \[ T(n)=8T(n/2)+\Theta(n^2)=\Theta(n^3). \]

Strassen’s Algorithm

Compute seven block products: \[ \begin{aligned} P_1&=A_{11}(B_{12}-B_{22}),&P_2&=(A_{11}+A_{12})B_{22},\\ P_3&=(A_{21}+A_{22})B_{11},&P_4&=A_{22}(B_{21}-B_{11}),\\ P_5&=(A_{11}+A_{22})(B_{11}+B_{22}),&P_6&=(A_{12}-A_{22})(B_{21}+B_{22}),\\ P_7&=(A_{11}-A_{21})(B_{11}+B_{12}). \end{aligned} \] Then \[ \begin{aligned} C_{11}&=P_5+P_4-P_2+P_6,&C_{12}&=P_1+P_2,\\ C_{21}&=P_3+P_4,&C_{22}&=P_1+P_5-P_3-P_7. \end{aligned} \] Thus \(T(n)=7T(n/2)+\Theta(n^2)=\Theta(n^{\log_2 7})\), about \(\Theta(n^{2.807})\). Pad with zeros to the next power of two when necessary; the dimension grows by less than a factor of two.

Convolution and FFT

Time Domain and Frequency Domain

Fourier analysis represents a signal as a combination of frequencies. In this chapter the algorithmic problem is converting polynomial coefficients into evaluations at roots of unity, and back.

Euler’s identity is \(e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\). A discrete Fourier transform uses finitely many samples; it is not the same statement as an infinite Fourier series.

Coefficient Representation

Let \[ A(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_jx^j,\qquad B(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b_jx^j. \]

  • Addition: add corresponding coefficients in \(\Theta(n)\) time.
  • Evaluation: Horner’s rule evaluates at one point in \(\Theta(n)\) time.
  • Multiplication: the product has degree at most \(2n-2\), with \[ c_k=\sum_{\substack{0\leq j<n\\0\leq k-j<n}}a_jb_{k-j}. \] This coefficient sequence is the convolution of \(a\) and \(b\). Direct convolution takes \(\Theta(n^2)\) arithmetic operations.

Point-Value Representation

A polynomial of degree at most \(d\) is uniquely determined by its values at \(d+1\) distinct points. In particular, a degree-at-most-\(n-1\) polynomial needs \(n\) points.

At shared sample points, addition and multiplication are pointwise: \[ (A+B)(x_j)=A(x_j)+B(x_j),\qquad (AB)(x_j)=A(x_j)B(x_j). \] Each takes linear time in the number of samples. However, \(n\) evaluations are insufficient to reconstruct a general degree-\(2n-2\) product: use at least \(2n-1\) distinct points. Evaluating at an arbitrary new point from point-value data is not generally constant time.

Roots of Unity and the DFT

Choose a power of two \(N\) and let \(\omega_N=e^{2\pi i/N}\). The \(N\)th roots are \(1,\omega_N,\ldots,\omega_N^{N-1}\).

For a coefficient vector padded to length \(N\), the positive-sign DFT convention is \[ y_k=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}a_j\omega_N^{jk},\qquad 0\leq k<N. \] The inverse uses the negative sign and divides by \(N\): \[ a_j=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}y_k\omega_N^{-jk}. \] The opposite sign convention is also valid if the inverse is changed consistently.

Fast Fourier Transform

Split the polynomial into even and odd coefficients: \[ A(x)=A_{\mathrm{even}}(x^2)+xA_{\mathrm{odd}}(x^2). \] The identities \(\omega_N^2=\omega_{N/2}\) and \(\omega_N^{k+N/2}=-\omega_N^k\) allow two half-size transforms to produce all \(N\) values. If their results are \(E_k\) and \(O_k\), \[ y_k=E_k+\omega_N^kO_k,\qquad y_{k+N/2}=E_k-\omega_N^kO_k. \] Therefore \(T(N)=2T(N/2)+\Theta(N)=\Theta(N\log N)\).

Polynomial Multiplication by FFT

  1. Choose a power of two \(N\geq2n-1\) and zero-pad both coefficient vectors.
  2. FFT both vectors.
  3. Multiply corresponding evaluations.
  4. Apply the inverse FFT and retain the product coefficients.

Total arithmetic complexity is \(O(n\log n)\). Without sufficient padding, the transform computes circular convolution and high-degree coefficients wrap around. Floating-point implementations also need numerical care; the arithmetic-operation bound is not an unrestricted bit-complexity bound.

Exam Transfer: Binary Search as Divide and Conquer

Rotated Sorted Array (2022 SAQ1)

For distinct values, maintain \([l,r]\) containing the minimum. Let \(m=\lfloor(l+r)/2\rfloor\). If \(A[m]>A[r]\), set \(l=m+1\); otherwise set \(r=m\). Stop at \(l=r\).

Invariant. Comparing with the right endpoint identifies which side contains the rotation point. Each iteration preserves the minimum and at least halves the interval: \(T(n)=T(n/2)+O(1)=O(\log n)\). Distinctness matters; duplicates can prevent this elimination.

Local Minimum (2025 Q4)

Treat missing outside neighbors as \(+\infty\). Check a midpoint against its original immediate neighbors. If it is smaller than both, return it. Otherwise recurse toward a smaller neighbor.

Correctness. Maintain an interval whose left boundary value is smaller than its outside left neighbor, and whose right boundary value is smaller than its outside right neighbor. The initial array satisfies this using the sentinels. Moving left preserves the right-boundary condition because \(A[m-1]<A[m]\); moving right is symmetric. A minimum inside the retained interval must then be a local minimum of the original array. Distinct values ensure strict inequalities. Only one half is searched, so the time is \(O(\log n)\).

Answer checklist. State the invariant, prove the discarded half is unnecessary, give the base case, and analyze index operations rather than copying array slices.
